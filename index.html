Scientific basis and interpretation

Absorbance, A = −log₁₀(T), is dimensionless; this relation applies to absorbance data, not to every normalized or ratio signal. For a dimensionless ordinate differentiated n times with respect to wavelength in nm, the derivative has units nm⁻ⁿ; other signal units are retained and multiplied by nm⁻ⁿ. Derivatives are estimated by local polynomial fitting at the recorded wavelengths, rather than by differentiation of an exact continuous spectrum. Their amplitudes and zero crossings depend on noise, sampling, polynomial degree, fitting-window width and boundary treatment, particularly at higher orders. Extrema, zero-crossing candidates, integrated areas and spectral overlays are numerical or graphical descriptors whose interpretation requires suitable controls and experimental context. Original measurements are preserved; missing-data gaps are not bridged in the plotted curves, and any interpolation in an aligned CSV is explicitly identified. Analytical selectivity, analyte identity and method validation require independent experimental evidence. PNG dimensions and DPI describe export settings; SVG preserves vector geometry independently of raster resolution.
